Compensation Approval

Compensation decisions need a disciplined process before the board reaches a number.

Attorney Review Required

This page summarizes legal-sensitive rules. Treat it as an issue-spotting draft until qualified legal counsel confirms the citations, thresholds, exceptions, and wording.

Educational Information Only

This field manual is general educational information for California board members. It is not legal advice, does not create an attorney-client relationship, and may not fit your organization's facts, governing documents, tax status, funding, or current law. Ask qualified counsel about specific decisions.

The boardroom problem

The executive director wants a raise, a founder receives benefits, or an officer’s role has changed. Directors may want to be fair and practical, but compensation can raise conflict, tax, disclosure, and fiduciary questions.

The careful rule

The board should use a disinterested, documented process supported by relevant comparability information. This page does not decide reasonableness, private benefit, employment classification, or tax reporting.

What the board should ask

Ask who is independent, what data supports the decision, whether benefits are included, how the decision will appear on Form 990, and what the minutes should record.

What not to do

Do not let the compensated person drive the approval process. Do not approve compensation because “we trust them” without documenting the basis for the decision.

When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when insiders, founders, directors, officers, key employees, family members, unusual benefits, or prior-process gaps are involved.

Source Notes

  • Attorney General's Guide for Charities - California Attorney General Accessed May 19, 2026.

Legal Escalation

When to call counsel

Call counsel before approving founder, executive, officer, director, or related-party compensation when independence or tax issues may exist.

  • The compensated person is a founder, director, officer, key employee, relative, or related party.
  • The board lacks comparability data or is unsure who is disinterested.
  • Benefits, deferred compensation, severance, housing, loans, or bonuses are involved.
  • Prior compensation was approved without a documented process.

Ask Before Voting

  • Who is disinterested for this decision?
  • What comparable data supports the amount and benefits?
  • Will the minutes show the process without unnecessary personnel detail?

Minutes Should Show

  • Who participated and who was absent or recused.
  • The comparability information considered.
  • The action taken and vote result.
