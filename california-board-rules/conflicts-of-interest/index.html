Conflicts of Interest

A conflict policy is only useful if the board follows it before the decision is made.

Attorney Review Required

It is not legal advice, does not create an attorney-client relationship, and may not fit your organization's facts, governing documents, tax status, funding, or current law. Ask qualified counsel about specific decisions.

The boardroom problem

The board has a conflict policy, but the real question appears during a live agenda item: a director’s company wants the contract, an officer’s relative is applying, or a founder has influence over a transaction.

The careful rule

Use conflict materials as process prompts, not automatic answers. California charity fiduciary duties, corporate rules, federal tax concepts, Form 990 disclosures, bylaws, and the organization’s own policy can all matter.

What the board should ask

Ask who is interested, what facts are known, whether the policy requires disclosure or recusal, whether comparable alternatives exist, and whether the minutes will show a disinterested process.

What not to do

Do not let the interested person manage the process. Do not rely on annual disclosure forms if the current transaction raises new facts.

When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when the board is approving a transaction, compensation, loan, lease, grant, employment relationship, or other benefit involving insiders or related parties.

Source Notes

  • Attorney General's Guide for Charities - California Attorney General Accessed May 19, 2026.
  • Instructions for Form 990 - Internal Revenue Service Accessed May 19, 2026.

Legal Escalation

When to call counsel

Call counsel before acting on conflicted transactions, insider benefits, related-party arrangements, or disputed recusals.

  • A director, officer, founder, executive, relative, donor, landlord, or vendor may benefit.
  • The board is unsure whether disclosure, recusal, comparability, or approval standards apply.
  • A conflict was discovered after approval.
  • The conflict involves compensation, property, loans, grants, or a major contract.

Ask Before Voting

  • Who may benefit directly or indirectly from this decision?
  • Has the interested person disclosed enough facts for the board to evaluate the conflict?
  • What does the policy require for discussion, recusal, approval, and minutes?

Minutes Should Show

  • The disclosed interest.
  • Who left the room or abstained, if applicable.
  • The disinterested decision-making process and vote result.
