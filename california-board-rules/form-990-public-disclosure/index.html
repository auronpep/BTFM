Form 990 and Public Disclosure

The Form 990 is not only a tax filing. It is a public document that can describe how the board governs.

Attorney Review Required

This page summarizes legal-sensitive rules. Treat it as an issue-spotting draft until qualified legal counsel confirms the citations, thresholds, exceptions, and wording.

Educational Information Only

This field manual is general educational information for California board members. It is not legal advice, does not create an attorney-client relationship, and may not fit your organization's facts, governing documents, tax status, funding, or current law. Ask qualified counsel about specific decisions.

The boardroom problem

The board thinks Form 990 belongs to the accountant. But Form 990 can disclose governance, compensation, transactions, fundraising, public support, and whether the organization makes documents available.

The careful rule

IRS guidance says exempt organizations generally must file annual returns, subject to exceptions, and warns that missing required filings for three consecutive years can cause automatic loss of tax-exempt status. The Form 990 instructions also include governance and disclosure questions that should match the organization’s real process.

What the board should ask

Ask who reviewed the draft, whether the return matches the audit and financial statements, which governance policies are referenced, and whether the board understands public-facing answers before filing.

What not to do

Do not approve a tax return you have not read. Do not let a policy exist only for the Form 990 answer. Do not assume public disclosure questions are harmless because the form is annual and familiar.

When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when disclosure answers may be inaccurate, filing status is uncertain, compensation or insider transactions appear, or the board is correcting missed filings.

Legal Escalation

When to call counsel

Call counsel when public disclosures, governance answers, compensation, transactions, or filing status are uncertain.

  • The return discloses compensation, insider transactions, grants, fundraising, or governance practices that may be misunderstood.
  • The organization has missed returns or may be at risk of automatic revocation.
  • The board wants to change a public answer but is unsure what the facts support.
  • California Registry filings rely on the same Form 990.

Ask Before Voting

  • Which board members reviewed the draft before filing?
  • Do governance answers match actual practice, not just policy language?
  • Are compensation, related-party, fundraising, and public-support disclosures understood by the board?

Minutes Should Show

  • Whether the board or a committee reviewed the Form 990 before filing.
  • Any questions, corrections, or follow-up assignments.
  • The process used to approve or acknowledge filing.
