$2M Audit Threshold

A board near the California audit threshold should treat audit planning as governance work, not an accounting surprise.

Attorney Review Required

This page summarizes legal-sensitive rules. Treat it as an issue-spotting draft until qualified legal counsel confirms the citations, thresholds, exceptions, and wording.

Educational Information Only

This field manual is general educational information for California board members. It is not legal advice, does not create an attorney-client relationship, and may not fit your organization's facts, governing documents, tax status, funding, or current law. Ask qualified counsel about specific decisions.

The boardroom problem

The board is reviewing a larger budget and everyone assumes the auditor, CFO, or outside accountant will say if an audit is required. That is too late. For an established California charity, the audit question affects the board calendar, committee structure, financial controls, Form 990 timing, and public credibility.

The careful rule

California Attorney General materials describe an audit requirement for covered charitable corporations with gross revenues of $2 million or more, with important details and exceptions. This page does not decide whether your organization is covered or whether a receipt counts toward the threshold.

Use the threshold as an agenda prompt: if the organization may be near it, put audit planning on the board calendar and verify the current rule with qualified advisors.

What the board should do

Ask management to show the revenue calculation, the prior year trend, and any categories that may need special treatment. Then confirm who is responsible for auditor selection, audit committee work, management-letter follow-up, and board review.

What not to do

Do not treat the $2 million number as a casual benchmark. Do not wait until the tax return is nearly due. Do not let the same people who prepare the financial story control the entire audit review process.

When this becomes legal

This becomes a legal and compliance question when the organization is close to the threshold, missed prior years, has complex receipts, or needs to know whether an exception applies.

Source Notes

  • Audit Requirements under the Nonprofit Integrity Act - California Attorney General Accessed May 19, 2026.
  • Attorney General's Guide for Charities - California Attorney General Accessed May 19, 2026.

Legal Escalation

When to call counsel

Call counsel when the audit obligation turns on revenue classification, exemptions, delinquency, or board structure.

  • Gross revenue is near or above $2 million and the board is unsure which receipts count.
  • The organization has government grants, restricted funds, related entities, or unusual transactions.
  • An audit was missed, delayed, or completed without board-level audit committee review.
  • The board wants to rely on an exception or correct prior noncompliance.

Ask Before Voting

  • Are we counting the revenue category the same way the California audit requirement counts it?
  • Does the organization have any government grants or other facts that may affect the threshold analysis?
  • Has the board calendar left enough time for auditor selection, fieldwork, committee review, and board acceptance?

Minutes Should Show

  • The materials reviewed to assess audit readiness.
  • The board or committee action taken on auditor selection or audit planning.
  • Any direction to verify the threshold and exceptions with counsel or the auditor.
