## The boardroom problem

The board thinks Form 990 belongs to the accountant. But Form 990 can disclose governance, compensation, transactions, fundraising, public support, and whether the organization makes documents available.

## The careful rule

IRS guidance says exempt organizations generally must file annual returns, subject to exceptions, and warns that missing required filings for three consecutive years can cause automatic loss of tax-exempt status. The Form 990 instructions also include governance and disclosure questions that should match the organization's real process.

## What the board should ask

Ask who reviewed the draft, whether the return matches the audit and financial statements, which governance policies are referenced, and whether the board understands public-facing answers before filing.

## What not to do

Do not approve a tax return you have not read. Do not let a policy exist only for the Form 990 answer. Do not assume public disclosure questions are harmless because the form is annual and familiar.

## When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when disclosure answers may be inaccurate, filing status is uncertain, compensation or insider transactions appear, or the board is correcting missed filings.
