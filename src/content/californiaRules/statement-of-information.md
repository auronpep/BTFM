## The boardroom problem

The board elects officers, changes addresses, or changes agents, but public records lag behind. That can create practical problems when notices, service of process, bank records, or public searches rely on stale information.

## The careful rule

California Secretary of State FAQ materials describe periodic Statement of Information filing obligations, including biennial filings for California charitable corporations. The exact filing window and entity record should be checked directly with the Secretary of State.

## What the board should ask

Ask whether the public record matches current board action, who receives filing reminders, and whether officer elections trigger a state-record check.

## What not to do

Do not confuse third-party solicitations with official filing obligations. Do not let former officers or stale addresses remain on public records without checking whether an update is needed.

## When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when state status is impaired, authority is disputed, notices were missed, or service of process is involved.
