## The boardroom problem

The finance committee says the numbers are fine. The auditor presents a clean report. The board moves on. But California audit committee rules may require a more specific board structure and a more independent review process.

## The careful rule

California Attorney General audit materials say qualifying charitable corporations must maintain an audit committee and describe limits on who may serve. The details matter. Committee composition should be checked against current official guidance, bylaws, and the organization's facts.

## What the board should ask

Ask whether the committee is appointed by the board, whether membership is independent enough for the required role, whether the auditor has access to the committee, and whether findings are tracked after the presentation.

## What not to do

Do not assume a finance committee automatically satisfies audit committee duties. Do not let management be the only filter between the auditor and the board.

## When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when the board needs to know whether its committee structure complies with California requirements, whether past audits were handled correctly, or whether bylaws need amendment.
