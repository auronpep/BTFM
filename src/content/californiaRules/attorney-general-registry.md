## The boardroom problem

The organization files tax returns, receives donations, and operates programs, but no one at the board table knows its California Registry status. That is a governance gap.

## The careful rule

California Attorney General materials say charitable corporations, unincorporated associations, charitable trustees, and other legal entities holding property for charitable purposes must register, and that registration is followed by annual renewal and reporting requirements. The details depend on entity type, activity, exemptions, and filing history.

## What the board should ask

Ask for current Registry status, the next renewal deadline, the forms used last year, and any notices received. Make one officer, committee, or staff role responsible for bringing status to the board annually.

## What not to do

Do not assume IRS filing means California Registry obligations are complete. Do not ignore notices because a preparer handled the tax return.

## When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when status is delinquent, uncertain, exempt, multistate, tied to fundraising, or connected to a transaction, dissolution, or asset transfer.
