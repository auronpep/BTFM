## The boardroom problem

The board is reviewing a larger budget and everyone assumes the auditor, CFO, or outside accountant will say if an audit is required. That is too late. For an established California charity, the audit question affects the board calendar, committee structure, financial controls, Form 990 timing, and public credibility.

## The careful rule

California Attorney General materials describe an audit requirement for covered charitable corporations with gross revenues of $2 million or more, with important details and exceptions. This page does not decide whether your organization is covered or whether a receipt counts toward the threshold.

Use the threshold as an agenda prompt: if the organization may be near it, put audit planning on the board calendar and verify the current rule with qualified advisors.

## What the board should do

Ask management to show the revenue calculation, the prior year trend, and any categories that may need special treatment. Then confirm who is responsible for auditor selection, audit committee work, management-letter follow-up, and board review.

## What not to do

Do not treat the $2 million number as a casual benchmark. Do not wait until the tax return is nearly due. Do not let the same people who prepare the financial story control the entire audit review process.

## When this becomes legal

This becomes a legal and compliance question when the organization is close to the threshold, missed prior years, has complex receipts, or needs to know whether an exception applies.
