## The boardroom problem

The board talks for two hours and the minutes say either too much or too little. Later, no one can tell what was approved, who had authority, or whether a conflict was handled.

## The careful rule

Minutes should create a reliable record of board action. California corporate law, bylaws, membership rights, regulator expectations, and litigation context can affect what records matter and who may access them.

## What the board should ask

Ask what decision was made, what document was reviewed, who was present, whether quorum existed, and what follow-up was assigned.

## What not to do

Do not use minutes as storytelling. Do not bury the motion in narrative. Do not include privileged or sensitive discussion without considering whether a different record process is needed.

## When this becomes legal

This becomes legal when minutes are disputed, requested, needed for a filing or transaction, tied to a conflict or compensation decision, or connected to litigation or regulator inquiry.
