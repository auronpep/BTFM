Reports matter, but listening to reports is not the board's highest use of meeting time. Directors should read routine reports before the meeting so the meeting can focus on judgment.

The rule is that every agenda item should have a purpose. Is the board receiving information, asking questions, giving direction, approving a motion, or documenting an executive session? If directors cannot tell, the agenda is making governance harder.

A better meeting starts with the packet. Reports come early. Decision items are labeled. Consent items are used carefully. The chair protects time for questions that actually affect board action.
