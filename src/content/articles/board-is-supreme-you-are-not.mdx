Nonprofit boards act as boards. Individual directors do not carry board authority around the organization simply because they have fiduciary duties.

The rule is that authority should be traceable. A director may ask questions, review materials, deliberate, dissent, and vote. But instructions to staff, contract approvals, policy decisions, public statements, and committee action need a source of authority.

This distinction protects everyone. It protects management from conflicting instructions. It protects directors from accidentally overstepping. It protects the organization by making decisions come through a process the minutes can show.
