A startup board can survive on urgency, trust, and a few people doing everything. A working board cannot. Once the organization has staff, regular funding, contracts, insurance, filings, and public expectations, board work must become more visible and repeatable.

The practical rule is simple: the board should be able to explain what it approved, what it relied on, and who had authority to act. That does not mean the board manages every detail. It means the board stops confusing trust with absence of oversight.

Good working-board habits are ordinary: timely packets, clear motions, committee reports tied to actual authority, conflict checks before votes, and minutes that show the decision trail. If those habits feel heavy, the organization may still be operating with startup instincts.
