Minutes are not a newsletter, transcript, or public relations document. They are the board's evidence that a meeting happened and that action was taken.

The rule is that minutes should be clear enough to prove the decision without recording every comment. Too much narrative can create confusion. Too little action detail can make it impossible to tell what passed.

Directors should review minutes as governance records. The question is not whether the minutes sound polished. The question is whether they show the meeting, the motion, the vote, the conflicts, and the materials the board relied on.
