When a board approves a budget, it is giving management permission to operate inside a financial plan. The board is not approving every future expense one at a time, but it is also not handing over a blank check.

The rule is that the budget should make authority visible. Directors should know what assumptions support the numbers, where the risk sits, and what kind of deviation brings the issue back to the board. A budget with no variance rule forces the board to guess later whether management stayed within authority.

A practical budget conversation does not require every director to become an accountant. It requires directors to ask whether the packet shows the story behind the numbers: revenue confidence, cash timing, restricted funds, staffing pressure, reserves, and the decisions management wants the board to authorize.
