Boardroom scenarios

Practice the moment before the board acts.

Scenarios turn common meeting pressure into a short decision path: what the board sees, where the risk sits, what to ask, what to avoid, and what the minutes should preserve.

Next meeting

next meeting

The Board Packet Arrived Too Late

The board is under pressure to approve a decision without enough time to review the materials.

Management says the deadline is real and delay would be inconvenient or embarrassing.

Money and audit

money audit

The Finance Committee Says Everything Is Fine

The full board is being asked to rely on committee confidence instead of understandable financial information.

Non-finance directors do not want to look uninformed, so they stay quiet.

Executive oversight

executive oversight

A Director Keeps Contacting Staff

Individual director activity is blurring board authority, staff supervision, and executive director accountability.

The director says they are only trying to help and that staff appreciate the attention.

executive oversight

The Executive Director Wants a Raise

The board must handle compensation with respect, process, comparability, conflict awareness, and clear records.

Directors value the executive director and worry that asking process questions will seem insulting.

Risk and safety

risk safety

The Board Has Not Reviewed Insurance in Years

The board cannot tell whether coverage matches current programs, contracts, people, and risks.

Insurance feels technical, and directors assume the broker or staff would raise any serious issue.

Minutes and records

minutes records

The Minutes Are Long, But No One Knows What Passed

The board record is verbose but weak as evidence of action.

Directors hesitate to revise the minutes because they do not want to reopen old debate.
