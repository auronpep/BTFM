The Executive Director Wants a Raise

A respected executive director requests a compensation increase and directors want to avoid an awkward conversation.

The boardroom problem

The board must handle compensation with respect, process, comparability, conflict awareness, and clear records.

Directors value the executive director and worry that asking process questions will seem insulting.

Recommended board response

  1. Move the discussion into the board process used for executive compensation.
  2. Confirm who is conflicted, who participates, and what comparability information is available.
  3. Use executive session where appropriate and document the action carefully.

What the board should ask

  • What process has the board used before?
  • What comparability information supports the proposed amount?
  • Who should be recused from the discussion or vote?

What not to do

  • Do not approve compensation casually at the end of a meeting.
  • Do not let the executive director participate in the board's final compensation decision.
  • Do not rely only on personal appreciation or fear of losing the executive.

Documents to pull

  • Executive compensation policy or prior process : Use the board's established procedure if one exists.
  • Comparable compensation information : Use appropriate comparability information for role, size, geography, and complexity.

Minutes should show

  • The process used, materials reviewed, recusals, and final board action.
  • Enough detail to show discipline without becoming a personnel transcript.

Red flags

  • The executive director drafts or controls the board's compensation record.
  • No comparability information is reviewed.
  • A conflicted director participates in the decision.

When this becomes legal

Call counsel when executive compensation, conflicts, intermediate sanctions, employment agreements, or California charity rules may affect the process.

  • The proposed compensation is unusually high or contested.
  • A related-party or conflict issue exists.
  • The board lacks a compensation approval process.

Boardroom notes

Executive compensation is both human and procedural. The board can value the executive director while still using a disciplined process.

The safest boardroom habit is to slow down, identify the process, gather comparability information, manage conflicts, and document the decision without unnecessary personnel narrative.
