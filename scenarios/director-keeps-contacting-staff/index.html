A Director Keeps Contacting Staff

A well-intentioned director bypasses the executive director and gives staff direct requests and instructions.

The boardroom problem

Individual director activity is blurring board authority, staff supervision, and executive director accountability.

The director says they are only trying to help and that staff appreciate the attention.

Recommended board response

  1. Confirm the board's staff-contact and information-request expectations.
  2. Route operational questions through the executive director or approved board channel.
  3. Document any board-approved delegation or limits.

What the board should ask

  • Is the director asking for information or directing work?
  • What channel should directors use for staff information requests?
  • Does this require a board policy or chair intervention?

What not to do

  • Do not let individual directors supervise staff informally.
  • Do not solve the problem through side conversations only.
  • Do not ignore staff confusion about who has authority.

Documents to pull

  • Bylaws and board policies : Check authority, officer roles, committee roles, and staff-contact policy.
  • Executive director job description : Clarify who supervises staff and how board requests move through management.

Minutes should show

  • Any board-approved staff-contact policy or authority clarification.
  • Any delegated follow-up to the chair or governance committee.

Red flags

  • Staff receive conflicting instructions from directors and management.
  • The director contacts staff about personnel, discipline, finance, or safety matters.
  • The executive director is excluded from board-staff communications.

When this becomes legal

Call counsel when director conduct affects employment, harassment, retaliation, investigations, authority, removal, or governance disputes.

  • Staff report pressure, retaliation, or inappropriate conduct.
  • The board may need to restrict or remove a director.

Boardroom notes

Helpful directors can still create governance problems. Staff need clarity about who supervises them, and the executive director needs a coherent management chain.

The board should turn the issue into policy and process, not personality. Clear channels protect staff, the executive director, and directors.
