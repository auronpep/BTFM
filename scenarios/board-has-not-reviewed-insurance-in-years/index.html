The Board Has Not Reviewed Insurance in Years

A director asks about insurance after a program expansion and no one knows the coverage, exclusions, or renewal process.

The boardroom problem

The board cannot tell whether coverage matches current programs, contracts, people, and risks.

Insurance feels technical, and directors assume the broker or staff would raise any serious issue.

Recommended board response

  1. Ask for a board-level insurance summary, not the entire policy stack first.
  2. Compare coverage to current programs, contracts, locations, vehicles, events, and vulnerable populations.
  3. Set an annual insurance review item before renewal.

What the board should ask

  • What has changed since coverage was last reviewed?
  • Which programs, locations, events, or populations create the highest exposure?
  • What incidents must be reported to the carrier and by when?

What not to do

  • Do not assume last year's coverage fits this year's programs.
  • Do not treat insurance as only a staff purchasing task.
  • Do not ignore exclusions, deductibles, and reporting duties.

Documents to pull

  • Insurance schedule or broker summary : List coverage types, limits, deductibles, exclusions, carriers, and renewal dates.
  • Program and contract inventory : Compare actual operations to covered risk.

Minutes should show

  • Coverage areas reviewed and follow-up assigned.
  • Any board direction for quotes, limits, policy changes, or budget impact.

Red flags

  • No director can explain basic coverage.
  • Programs expanded into new risks without insurance review.
  • Incident reporting duties are unclear.

When this becomes legal

Call counsel when coverage, contracts, incidents, investigations, youth or vulnerable-population safety, or regulatory duties may affect board action.

  • A serious incident occurred or may need reporting.
  • A contract requires insurance the organization may not have.

Boardroom notes

Insurance is technical, but the board question is plain: does coverage match the work the organization is actually doing?

The board does not need to read every policy line at every meeting. It does need a repeatable review rhythm, a clear summary, and escalation when programs, contracts, or incidents create legal exposure.
