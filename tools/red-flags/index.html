Board Red Flags

Red flags are not accusations. They are signals that directors need better facts, clearer authority, stronger minutes, or professional advice before the organization relies on a decision.

These tools are educational board-preparation materials. They do not provide legal advice, do not create an attorney-client relationship, and should be escalated to counsel when facts, filings, disputes, or legal rights matter.

Signals

Slow down when the signal changes the decision

urgent

Conflict Handled Informally

Signal: A director, officer, insider, vendor, or family relationship may benefit from a board decision.

Why it matters: Conflicts can affect process, records, public trust, and legal review.

Immediate board action

  • Identify the interest before discussion and vote.
  • Confirm whether recusal, abstention, or counsel review is needed.
  • Document the process in minutes.

urgent

Missing Motion Document

Signal: The agenda includes approval of a policy, budget, contract, filing, or minutes item without the actual version attached.

Why it matters: A future record should identify what the board approved. Missing documents make authority and evidence unclear.

Immediate board action

  • Request the exact version before approval.
  • Defer the vote if the version is not available.
  • Make the minutes identify the approved version.

urgent

Safety Issue Minimized

Signal: The board learns of a safety concern but no one identifies reporting, insurance, personnel, or legal follow-up.

Why it matters: Safety issues can become claims, reporting duties, reputational harm, or insurance problems.

Immediate board action

  • Ask what immediate protective steps have been taken.
  • Ask whether insurer, counsel, HR, or law enforcement advice is needed.
  • Document board-level follow-up without exposing unnecessary details.

serious

Individual Director Acting Alone

Signal: A director bypasses the board or executive leadership to control operations.

Why it matters: The board acts collectively. Individual action can create confusion, liability, and management dysfunction.

Immediate board action

  • Clarify board, officer, committee, and executive authority.
  • Use the Board Authority Map.
  • Record any delegation or boundary decision.

serious

Late Board Packet

Signal: Directors receive a large or consequential packet shortly before the meeting.

Why it matters: Late packets can convert board approval into rubber-stamping and weaken the record of informed attention.

Immediate board action

  • Ask which items can be informational only.
  • Defer material votes when directors lack enough time.
  • Record follow-up when the board proceeds for a specific reason.

serious

Material Budget Gap

Signal: A deficit, restricted-fund issue, revenue shortfall, or expense increase is described vaguely.

Why it matters: The board may need to approve changes, monitor reserves, or understand program commitments.

Immediate board action

  • Ask whether the gap is timing, assumption, control, or strategy.
  • Request a cash-flow or reserve explanation where relevant.
  • Assign follow-up before the next board meeting.
